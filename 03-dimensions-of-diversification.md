# Dimensions of diversification

Diversification works along several dimensions at once: across asset classes, across sectors, and across geographies. Spreading along more than one of these dimensions lowers the chance that a single force, an industry slump, a country's troubles, or a stock-market crash, drags your whole portfolio down together. Each dimension addresses a different kind of shared risk.

## Across asset classes

Asset classes are broad categories like stocks, bonds, real estate, and cash. They tend to respond differently to the same conditions: bonds often hold up or rise when stocks fall, and cash stays stable when both wobble. Holding more than one asset class is usually the most powerful form of diversification because the classes are driven by different forces. A portfolio of only stocks is exposed to the stock market alone, however many stocks it holds.

## Across sectors

Within stocks, sectors are groups of companies in the same line of business: technology, healthcare, energy, finance, consumer goods, and so on. Sectors rise and fall on their own cycles, so a portfolio concentrated in one sector carries that sector's specific risk. Spreading across sectors means a downturn in one area, say energy prices collapsing, does not take the whole stock portion with it. Sector diversification is what turns a pile of stocks into a genuinely broad holding.

## Across geographies

Geographic diversification means holding investments tied to different countries and regions. Economies, currencies, interest rates, and political conditions differ from place to place, so a problem concentrated in one country need not affect holdings elsewhere. An investor whose entire portfolio sits in a single national market is exposed to that one economy's fate. Spreading internationally adds protection against any single country's slump, though it introduces currency movements and differing local conditions as factors to be aware of.
